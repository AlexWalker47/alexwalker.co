Glass Stats Support

Glass Stats puts live CPU, GPU, memory and network meters in your Mac's menu bar. Each meter shows a small live graph and a number. Click one to open a Liquid Glass panel with history charts, per-core load, top processes and apps, GPU memory and network totals. Glass Stats has no Dock icon. It lives in the menu bar.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the process list show "System & other users"?
Apps from the Mac App Store run in Apple's sandbox, which limits what an app can read about other processes. Glass Stats can list your own processes one by one. CPU used by macOS itself (for example WindowServer and kernel_task) and by other users' processes is shown as one "System & other users" row.

Why do some helper processes show as separate rows?
The same sandbox limit applies. Grouping an app's helper processes under the app (for example Safari's web content processes) needs information the sandbox does not allow, so the helpers appear as their own rows. The memory list ranks apps by resident memory, so its numbers can differ a little from Activity Monitor.

How do I open Settings?
Click any Glass Stats meter in the menu bar, then click the gear button in the panel. You can also open Glass Stats again from the Applications folder.

How do I reset my settings?
Open Settings and choose Reset to Defaults. This puts the menu bar items, their order, refresh rate, glass strength and launch at login back to how they were at install.

How do I start Glass Stats when I log in?
Open Settings and turn on Launch at login. It is off by default. If macOS asks for approval, allow Glass Stats in System Settings › General › Login Items.

What does Glass Stats need?
macOS 26 (Tahoe) or later, on an Apple silicon or Intel Mac.

Does Glass Stats collect my data?
No. See the Privacy Policy.
